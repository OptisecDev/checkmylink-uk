How CheckMyLink UK works

When you paste a link, phone number, or message, we run it through several free, publicly available safety checks and combine the results into one simple answer: Safe, Use Caution, or Danger. Here's what each check actually does, in plain English.

URLhaus

A free, community-run list of web addresses known to spread malware (harmful software that can damage your device or steal your information). If your link is on this list, it's a confirmed danger.

OpenPhish

A free, community-run list of web addresses confirmed to be phishing sites - fake pages designed to look like a real bank or company so they can steal your login details or money.

Spamhaus DBL (Domain Block List)

A trusted, widely-used industry list of website addresses known to be used for spam, scams, or malware. We check whether the site's domain name appears on it.

WHOIS domain age

We check how long ago a website's address was first registered. Scam websites are often set up only days before they're used, then abandoned. A very new domain is not proof of a scam on its own, but it is a useful warning sign.

Typosquatting / brand impersonation detection

We compare the web address against a list of major UK banks, government services, and well-known brands. If it's a near-miss spelling of a real brand (like "barclay-secure.com" instead of "barclays.co.uk"), or the brand's name appears inside an unrelated web address, we flag it.

SSL certificate check

Most legitimate websites use a security certificate (shown as a padlock in your browser) to encrypt your connection. We check how old that certificate is - a brand-new certificate on a site that's also impersonating a well-known brand is an extra warning sign.

Phone number check

If you paste a UK phone number, or one appears inside a pasted message, we check it against a small, publicly-documented list of number patterns commonly linked to scam calls - such as premium-rate numbers, and international dialling codes often used in "missed call" callback scams.

URL shortener resolution

Scammers sometimes use link-shortening services (like bit.ly) to hide where a link really goes. When we spot one of these, we follow it - safely, with a strict time limit - to find the real destination, then run every check above against that real destination too.

No single check is perfect on its own, which is why we combine all of them into one overall result. Read about our limitations on the About page.
